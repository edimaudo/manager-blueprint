# Manager Blueprint — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Product Overview
**Manager Blueprint** is a manager development application that helps managers become better leaders through management knowledge, practical frameworks, tools and templates, assessments, real-world practice, and optional AI-powered coaching.

The initial product focus is **first-time managers**, while the underlying product is designed to expand to aspiring managers, experienced managers, senior managers, and directors/executives.

The product is not a resume tool, job tracker, course marketplace, community platform, or HR system.

### Core product promise
Help managers improve their ability to:
- Manage themselves
- Manage others
- Manage up
- Handle real management situations
- Build stronger management capabilities over time

## 2. Target Users
### Primary launch audience: First-time managers
Typical needs include:
- Transitioning from individual contributor to manager
- Delegating effectively
- Running effective 1:1s
- Giving feedback
- Coaching employees
- Managing performance
- Handling conflict
- Working effectively with their manager
- Preparing for skip-level and executive interactions
- Building confidence as a manager

### Future audiences
- Aspiring managers
- Experienced managers
- Senior managers
- Directors and executives

The onboarding and Blueprint should adapt to management level.

## 3. Core Management Domains
### Managing Yourself
Topics include productivity, prioritization, time management, decision making, communication, leadership mindset, personal effectiveness, and career growth.

### Managing Others
Topics include 1:1 meetings, delegation, coaching, feedback, conflict management, performance conversations, hiring, and employee development.

### Managing Up
Topics include working with your manager, stakeholder management, executive communication, influence, skip-level meetings, visibility, and career growth.

## 4. Core Product Experience
The user's experience is centered on their **Manager Blueprint**.

The Blueprint represents:
- Management stage
- Goals
- Strengths
- Development opportunities
- Recommended areas of focus
- Progress through development activities

It is personalized using onboarding information, goals/challenges, assessment results, and activity/progress information.

The Blueprint is the application experience. Tools, templates, practice, assessments, and AI are capabilities within it rather than separate products or primary navigation destinations.

## 5. Core Capability Types
### Knowledge
Frameworks, best practices, examples, and practical guidance.

### Tools and Templates
Practical resources such as 1:1 templates, feedback frameworks, delegation planners, checklists, conversation guides, productivity tools, stakeholder maps, and skip-level meeting planners.

### Practice
Realistic management situations such as giving feedback, delegating work, handling conflict, coaching an employee, managing expectations, and communicating with executives.

### Assessments
Baseline capability assessment, self-reflection, skill-specific assessments, and progress measurement.

### AI
Contextual manager coaching, role play, personalized feedback, and development recommendations.

## 6. Monetization and Access Model
Manager Blueprint has **two independent monetization dimensions**.

### Dimension A — Content access
#### Free access
Users can:
- Create a Manager Blueprint
- Complete the initial assessment
- Receive basic recommendations
- Use a limited set of free templates/resources
- Access starter guidance

#### One-time purchases
Users can permanently purchase individual premium resources or packaged products.

Examples:
- First-Time Manager Blueprint — $55
- Delegation Toolkit — $15
- Feedback Toolkit — $20
- Productivity Toolkit — $25

Pricing is illustrative and configurable.

### Dimension B — Subscription access
Subscription provides ongoing capabilities, primarily:
- AI Manager Coach
- AI role play
- Personalized feedback
- Ongoing development support
- Subscriber resources and/or continuously added resources, subject to final commercial policy

Subscription access does **not** require a prior one-time purchase.

## 7. Supported Customer Paths
The system must support all of these independently:
- Free → One-time purchase
- Free → Subscription
- One-time purchase → Subscription
- Free only
- One-time purchase only

A subscription and one-time purchases are not mutually exclusive.

The product must not force a linear Free → Purchase → Subscription funnel.

## 8. Access / Entitlement Principles
All users experience the same Manager Blueprint product.

Access is determined at the individual resource/capability level (resource-level entitlement).

### Free user
- Free knowledge/resources: available
- Premium purchased resources: locked
- Subscription capabilities: locked

### One-time purchaser
- Purchased content/resources: available
- Other premium products: locked
- Subscription capabilities: locked unless subscribed

### Subscriber
- Subscription capabilities: available
- Subscriber-included content/resources: available according to subscription policy
- Separately sold products remain governed by their defined entitlement policy

The UI must not create separate application areas for free users, purchasers, and subscribers.

## 9. Initial User Experience
### Screen 1 — Welcome
Primary message: **Become the manager people want to work for.**

Supporting message: Build the skills, confidence, and practical habits to become a better manager.

Primary CTA: **Create My Manager Blueprint**

Secondary CTA: **Sign In**

### Screen 2 — Management Stage
Ask: **Where are you in your management journey?**

Options:
- Aspiring Manager
- First-Time Manager
- Experienced Manager
- Senior Manager
- Director / Executive

First-time manager is the primary launch path.

### Screen 3 — Goals and Challenges
Users select development needs across Managing Yourself, Managing Others, and Managing Up.

### Screen 4 — Manager Assessment
Conduct a concise practical diagnostic across management capabilities. The assessment identifies strengths and development opportunities rather than only producing a generic personality score.

### Screen 5 — Your Manager Blueprint
Show management stage, strengths, development opportunities, and recommended starting point.

Example:
- Stage: First-Time Manager
- Strengths: Communication, Problem Solving
- Development opportunities: Delegation, Feedback, Managing Up

Primary CTA: **Start My Blueprint**

### Screen 6 — Access Options
Present three choices without implying a mandatory sequence:

**Start Free**
- Personalized Blueprint
- Basic guidance
- Free templates/resources

**Buy What You Need**
- One-time products
- Premium templates
- Toolkits
- Playbooks/frameworks

**Go Further**
- Subscription
- AI Coach
- AI role play
- Personalized feedback
- Ongoing development capabilities

Users can select any appropriate path.

## 10. High-Level Product UI
### Manager Blueprint Home
Provides:
- Current management stage
- Current development focus
- Recommended action
- Progress
- Access to the three management domains

### Management domain view
Contains:
- Managing Yourself
- Managing Others
- Managing Up

### Capability view
A capability such as Delegation can contain:
- Learn / knowledge
- Apply / tools and templates
- Practice / scenarios and role play
- Assess / measurement and reflection

Access indicators appear at the resource level.

### One-time purchase experience
Purchases should be contextual where possible. The purchase experience explains what is included, price, one-time ownership, and whether subscription access already includes it.

### Subscription experience
Subscription prompts appear when the user reaches subscription-gated capabilities such as AI coaching or AI role play. The user does not need to purchase a one-time product first.

## 11. Product Principles
1. The Blueprint is the product experience.
2. Do not create separate primary sections for AI, tools, templates, practice, or a library.
3. Do not treat the product as an LMS or generic template marketplace.
4. Organize around management capabilities and real manager needs.
5. Free, one-time purchase, and subscription are access states—not separate applications.
6. One-time purchases and subscription are independent paths.
7. AI is contextual, not a standalone destination.
8. First-time managers are the initial design priority.
9. Users should receive meaningful value before being asked to pay.
10. Premium access should be surfaced contextually at the point of need.
